Position Blinkmore as a playful screen break that invites comfortable, natural bilateral blinking and a brief rest from near work. Dry eye is multifactorial and the 2025 TFOS DEWS III diagnostic report requires both symptoms and clinical signs. Clinical assessment can include tear stability, ocular surface staining and eyelid structure. A webcam blink score cannot diagnose, prevent or treat dry eye. Appropriate copy is “take a blink break” or “check in with how your eyes feel,” not “we detect dry eye” or “this exercise cures dryness.” [TFOS DEWS III diagnostic methodology](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajo.2025.09.035), [National Eye Institute dry eye overview](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/dry-eye)

Blink practice has promising but bounded clinical evidence. A 2025 study optimized a specific close, squeeze, open routine in people with dry eye. Fifteen repetitions three times daily for two weeks reduced symptom scores, incomplete blinks and conjunctival staining in a small efficacy cohort, but most measures returned to baseline two weeks after stopping, and blink rate, tear breakup time and tear volume did not significantly change. That does not validate this app’s camera or a different playful routine. Keep the interaction optional and gentle: an animation can invite a few natural blinks, with no forced squeeze, one eye wink, long hold, or correction goal. [Optimisation of blinking exercises for dry eye disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40467388/)

With the existing 640 by 480, 15 frame per second pipeline, the defensible webcam observation is that each calibrated visible eyelid aperture signal crossed a closure threshold during the session. The app can report a “bilateral closure event” when both signals cross within a chosen time window. Do not call it a verified full or complete blink unless this exact system has been validated against independent expert labels and an appropriate clinical reference. A published video classifier used close, high resolution, near infrared images and expert adjudication, and still reported lower F1 for incomplete than complete blinks, 80.9 percent versus 95.3 percent. At 15 frames per second, samples are about 67 milliseconds apart, so describe timing as approximate. [Video Based Eye Blink Identification and Classification](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35213320/)

Do not infer gaze, pupil function, retinal health, dry eye severity, or a need to correct left right motion. The system has no pupil or gaze signal. Left and right aperture differences are an observation of this camera session, not a diagnosis of asymmetry or nerve dysfunction. Facial nerve palsy evaluation uses history and a broader examination of the face, eyelids, cornea, vision, eye movements and pupils. Retinal examination requires viewing the back of the eye through a dilated pupil. [AAO EyeWiki facial nerve palsy examination](https://eyewiki.aao.org/Facial_Nerve_Palsy), [NEI retinal detachment diagnosis](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/retinal-detachment)

For product telemetry, distinguish three things: a camera observed aperture threshold crossing, a timer that confirms a prompt was displayed for 20 seconds, and a user’s report of comfort. The latter two do not prove the user looked far away, blinked, or had symptom improvement. Evaluate detection quality against blinded human video labels, including misses, false events, timing error and robustness to lighting, glasses, head movement and occlusion. Session completion, opt out, stop rate and momentary self reported comfort are usability outcomes, not proof of health efficacy.

A 20 second look at a distant real object is a reasonable, low risk break prompt and matches NEI public guidance, but present it as a habit cue rather than an established treatment. Evidence for the exact 20 20 20 prescription is mixed: a 29 person webcam reminder study found reduced symptoms that were not maintained after reminders stopped and no change in ocular surface signs, while a separate 30 person experiment found no significant symptom effect from scheduled 20 second breaks. Before claiming that Blinkmore improves dry eye, study this exact app and routine prospectively against a suitable comparator in the intended population, with prespecified, clinically meaningful symptom and clinical sign endpoints such as OSDI 6, noninvasive tear breakup time and ocular surface staining, and follow up after stopping. TFOS trial guidance calls for outcome measures chosen in advance, adequate sample size and good quality controlled studies because dry eye signs and symptoms correlate weakly.
